# V2: the new look (work in progress on the `v2` branch)

V2 is the new look for all four builds: the Knomi, Mac, Windows and the web. It is being built on the `v2` branch,
cut from `knomi-v2` at 1.51, and stays there until it launches. The approved designs, mockups and the screen
checklist are in the roadmap session's notes (`v2-prep/`: `README.md`, `ASSETS.md`, `mockups/`, `review/`, `scripts/`).

## The switch

Everything new sits behind one build flag, `UI_V2`: `1` (the default on this branch, set in `ui_v2.h`) draws V2, `0`
draws 1.51's screens. Nothing in the save depends on it.

- With `UI_V2=0` the game compiles to exactly 1.51's code: the preprocessed sketch is identical, and the Knomi's
  `firmware.bin` comes out the same size as 1.51's.
- V2's code is in `scene_v2.h` (and the walk in `walk_v2.h`, which it includes), with the positions in `layout_v2.h`,
  the colours in `palettes_v2.h` and the scenery in `sprites_v2.h`. The sketch includes `scene_v2.h` just before
  `render()`, so it can use the drawing helpers. `sdmon.cpp` reads the flag too, for the walk's sprite rows.

| Build | V2 (default) | V1 |
|---|---|---|
| Knomi | `arduino-cli compile ... --build-property "compiler.cpp.extra_flags=-DBOARD_KNOMI_V2"` | add `-DUI_V2=0` to those flags |
| Mac | `zsh mac/build_app.sh` | `UI_V2=0 zsh mac/build_app.sh` (rebuilds the host library) |
| Windows | `zsh windows/build.sh` | `UI_V2=0 zsh windows/build.sh` |
| Web | `WEB_OUT=build/web-v2 zsh webgame/build.sh` | `UI_V2=0 WEB_OUT=build/web-v1 zsh webgame/build.sh` |

`WEB_OUT` keeps a test build out of `web/play`, which is what gets published. A copy served from localhost doesn't
join the buddy relay unless the address has `?relay=1`, just as Mac and Windows test copies need `POKEMACGOCHI_RELAY=1`.
The Wi-Fi is the other way in: a Mac or Windows copy, and the screenshot tool, talk to the Knomis on the same network
on their own port unless they run with `POKEMACGOCHI_BUDDY_PORT=47391`. With both, a test pet never turns up beside
the real ones at home. (The host tests switch the network off themselves.)

## For Fedor's review

Choices made while Fedor was away, each easy to change:

- **The walk's timing** (step 4): a walk of 10 to 20 s after a rest of at least 8 to 16 s.
- **The walk's stream** (step 4): while it walks, the streaming grass and flowers (the Beach's shells, the Cave's
  rocks) take the place of the still scene's ones on the near hill.
- **The time zone picker** (5a) keeps V1's arrows and OK, one city at a time; mockup #21 is a scrolling list.

## Fights that pay: the Master Ball (Fedor, 2026-11-07; after the graphics)

Fedor: "Right now when you are hunting for a specific pokemon, whether its to complete a zone, hunt for a shiny, or
just to get a 3 star pokemon, you do a lot of clicking on run. Only time you fight is when the run fails. What can we
give to the player or pokemon that would incentives completing fights?" His idea: "Maybe there is a bar that shows
progress towards a master ball (a ball that always lands a catch if the pokemon is hit) these would stack but you
could only earn them through fights. It should probably take you 20-30 fights to earn a single master ball. It should
feel super special and difficult to get. 20-30 depends if a casual player can get one in a single day. That should be
the goal."

- A bar fills with each fight fought to the end; when it's full the player earns a Master Ball and it starts again.
- Master Balls stack, and fights are the only way to earn them.
- A Master Ball always catches, as long as the throw hits the Pokemon.
- 20 to 30 fights per ball: set it so a casual player earns about one a day.
- It should feel special and hard to get.

Settled (Fedor, 2026-11-07, "Yes, use them"): a fight is any wild or Travel battle fought to the end, won or lost;
not running away, buddy battles or the dungeon. 25 per ball. The bar shows on the battle's end screen and on the
card. A Master Ball button beside the normal throw, only when you have one. A purple pixel ball with an M.

## The Repel (Fedor, 2026-11-07)

Fedor: "Repel. This is spray type icon ... that keeps away lower level/weaker pokemon during wild encounters. For
travel locations, if they are incomplete, it would even out the chances of all the non legendary pokemon to appear.
If they are complete, it would act the same as wild encounters. All of this would happen for a set amount of time.
Maybe 10 minutes to start. The repel would live as another item inside the backpack with the berries and candy.
Chances for shiny pokemon arent touched. Repel is earned much like the Master Ball except its done via playing mini
games. Each second played adds up. The total to earn one should be on average of 1 Repel per day. It would stack up
just like the MasterBall does."

Built (`repel_v2.h`, `pet.cpp`): a Repel runs 10 of the game's minutes (it runs out while the board is off, too).
Wild encounters, and Travel zones you've completed, bring no foe below your level and no species whose base stats
add up to less than yours (if that leaves nobody, the usual pick). A zone not yet complete gives every non-legendary
there the same chance (its legendary keeps its 1 in 100). Shiny odds are untouched. Every second of a minigame
actually played (not its card, not its end, not waiting for a buddy) counts; 300 earn a Repel. It's the backpack's
last seat while you hold one or one runs (its count; "10m" while running), "Repel: 10 min" shows on the home when one
starts, and the bag wears a purple badge with the minutes left. A game's end shows the spray, how many you hold and
"3:22/5:00" (or "REPEL!" for one just earned); the card's Progress page has it under the Master Ball. Saved as "rpl",
"rpls", "rplu"; the trainer's, up to 99.
